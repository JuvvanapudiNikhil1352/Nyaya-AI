### Re-opening of unfair loan transactions by a court (relevant_law)
In any suit brought for the recovery of a loan or enforcement of a loan agreement, a court may re-open the transaction if it has reason to believe that the interest is excessive and the transaction was substantially unfair. If both conditions are satisfied, the court may take an account between the parties and relieve you of liability for excessive interest.
Sources: The Usurious Loans Act, 1918, Section 3 (part 1 of 2); The Usurious Loans Act, 1918, Section 3 (part 2 of 2); The Usurious Loans Act, 1918, Section 2

### Determination of excessive interest and unfairness (possible_concern)
Interest is considered excessive if it is above what the court deems reasonable considering the lender's risk at the time of the loan. The court considers factors such as the presence or absence of security, your financial condition, and all circumstances affecting the relationship of the parties. High interest itself may be sufficient evidence that the transaction was substantially unfair.
Sources: The Usurious Loans Act, 1918, Section 3 (part 1 of 2); The Usurious Loans Act, 1918, Section 3 (part 2 of 2)

### Court powers to adjust loan terms or payments (possible_remedy)
